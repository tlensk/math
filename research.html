RESEARCH / QUESTIONS & APPROACH

Mathematics of structure, learning, and explanation.

My research is motivated by a recurring question: what makes the organization of complex systems discoverable, representable, and scientifically interpretable?

01 / MOTIVATION

From prediction toward understanding

My work has explored how computational models can recover meaningful organization from complex biological and clinical data. This includes developing scalable algorithms, selecting and evaluating predictive features, and comparing independent lines of evidence.

These experiences motivate a broader interest in how the structure of a data-generating process shapes learned representations—and what is required to establish that a computational explanation is faithful rather than merely plausible.

I approach these problems through mathematical reasoning, algorithm development, controlled experiments, and rigorous model validation.

CURRENT RESEARCH INTERESTS

01 — MODEL

Structured synthetic data

How can we construct tractable data-generating processes with known latent variables, dependencies, and hierarchical organization?

I am interested in graph-based and modular models, sparse representations, and controlled variations in data complexity. Such models can serve as experimental environments for examining learning.

Graph-based modelsHierarchical structureSynthetic benchmarks
02 — LEARN

Learned representations

Which properties of the data do neural networks learn, and how does the answer depend on architecture, objectives, data distribution, and scale?

My prior work in predictive modeling provides a methodological foundation for studying feature relationships and model behavior. I am extending these interests toward internal representations and mechanistic evaluation.

Representation learningFeature analysisNeural networks
03 — TEST

Validation & scientific explanation

When does an apparently meaningful feature or circuit correspond to a real computational mechanism?

I am interested in reproducible methods that compare recovered structures with explicit ground truth, controlled perturbations, and independent evidence.

02 / SCIENTIFIC CONTINUITY

What biological systems taught me about structure

In computational genomics, meaningful architectures are rarely visible through a single measurement. Their identification requires combining sequence signals, structural relationships, genomic context, and independent validation.

In biomedical machine learning, predictive utility similarly depends on appropriate variables, experimental design, and careful evaluation. A model can be useful without revealing the mechanism responsible for its behavior.

These are not claims that biological organization and artificial neural networks obey the same laws. They are methodological reasons to investigate structural questions across domains.
